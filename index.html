Company Brain

Not thousands of agents: one governed brain per enterprise, executed by a small team of agents.

Read the paper – All you need is a Company Brain PDF on Zenodo Watch the animations Try the POC

The idea

Enterprises are told they will run thousands of AI agents. The Company Brain starts from a different idea: what needs designing is not the agent but the enterprise's brain, a single governed graph of what the company knows, how it works and what it can do, with permissions, provenance and audit on every node.

A small team of agents with distinct roles executes it, acting on behalf of the user and holding only the privileges of the current phase. At night a governed process rereads the day's traces and proposes improvements, which go live only after tests and human sign-off.

The language model is the engine and can be replaced; the brain is the asset that remains the enterprise's own. What must grow into the thousands are its capabilities, not the agents, the identities or the permissions to govern.

Five pillars

  1. Capability graph

    Skills, tools, profiles and policies as versioned, signed nodes with typed relationships; the knowledge bases sit behind read-only MCP tools.

  2. Dynamic tools

    For every task, retrieval extracts only the bundle it needs, within a budget of tokens and exposed tools.

  3. Per-phase policy plane

    At every phase declared by an external state machine, deterministic rules materialize the profile and the privileges of each session.

  4. Small team

    Four archetypes — Orchestrator, Reader, Executor, Verifier — acting on behalf of the user, with no standing credentials per instance.

  5. Governed evolution

    At night, Dreaming proposes and tests changes from the day's traces; whatever grants authority changes only with human sign-off.

Proof of concept

The architecture at work

The POC puts the core of the paper to work on a business-document case: the small team, the Policy Plane that governs it phase by phase and the human approvals. You ask for a document; the team plans, writes and verifies it.

Open Company Brain

The team

Each agent has its own job and its own permissions. Above them sits the Policy Plane: rules outside the model that decide when the work moves to the next phase.

Orchestrator

Captain America

Reads the request and proposes the plan: assignments, phases and success criteria.

leads the team, never touches production

Reader

Scarlet Witch

Searches the authorized sources and brings back the citations.

reads minds; here, only sources

Executor

Iron Man

Writes the document from what the Reader has found.

suits up, but only with a token

Verifier

Doctor Strange

Checks the result with a different model, independently of whoever wrote it.

saw 14,000,605 futures; checks yours too

Policy Plane

Nick Fury

No superpowers and no model: only rules. Same input, same answer, every time. It decides phase by phase who may do what, keeps the budget and asks for your approval.

one eye, zero hallucinations

Orchestrator, Reader and Executor run on Claude; the Verifier on an OpenAI model. The Policy Plane uses no model: it is deterministic code.

How to try it

  1. Sign inOpen Company Brain and sign in with the Microsoft account enabled for the POC.
  2. Say what you needOne sentence is enough, for example “Instructions for a new developer's first week”.
  3. Approve the planThe team proposes assignments and phases. You can approve it, ask for changes or reject it: nothing starts without your approval.
  4. Follow the workThe monitor shows, phase by phase, what each agent is doing. At the end you receive the document, already verified.
